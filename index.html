Two layers, deliberately

Attribution is what makes dealers refuse. Planning does not need it.

Aggregate — on by default Total units and value landing per week, across every dealer. No names, no per-project detail. A factory schedule does not need to know whose job it is.
Project detail — dealer opts in, per project Named project, units, and whether the estimate is still being worked on. In exchange the dealer gets a committed lead time on that quote instead of “subject to capacity.”

Never shared, at either layer: who else the dealer is pricing, their margin, what they quote the builder, or any job they have not asked you to price.

How a code is read

Codes are generated from a grammar, not picked from a list. Page 70 defines it.
TTall cabinet 3636" wide 8484" high 2424" deep BButt doors
Where the grammar bites
Field widths have no delimiter. W241224B reads as 24×12×24 butt at 71%, or 2412×24B at 29%. Only a valid-dimension list settles it — and that list is not in the book.
An underscore changes the product. ROS is a field-installed roll-out shelf; _ROS is factory-installed. Different price, different routing. Three codes in the book are typeset ambiguously.
Part codes reverse their order. DR is width then height. DF is height then width. Same page.
