What the messy data means for what we can build

HAND-MARKED ORDERS

Notes can mean more than one thing

"F-L" could be finished left or hinge left. The product shows its readings and asks, instead of guessing.

CATALOG CODES

Some codes read two ways

W241224B has no separators between sizes. Only the manufacturer can confirm which reading is right.

PRICES

Not in the catalog at all

They live in a separate price book, times each dealer's discount. Without it, nothing prices automatically.

So full automation isn't a realistic promise. The product prices what's certain, stops on what isn't, and keeps every correction so the same problem isn't solved twice.

The assumption to confirm first

Faster quotes win more jobs. The question is how many.

How I'd check

Ask dealers, since they're the ones choosing:

  • Why did you choose that manufacturer on your last few jobs?
  • A firm price in 2 hours with one line open, or a complete price in 3 days?

If speed matters less than we think

Sell the same tool as a way to save work: the same team handles more quotes without hiring. That's a pitch to operations rather than sales.

What I'd want to learn first

DealersWhy did you choose that manufacturer? whether speed really is the selling point
ManufacturersWho prepares your quotes: your team, or reps? who the user actually is
ManufacturersWould you connect your price book? the full product, or a smaller one
ManufacturersWould you ask dealers to use a platform? whether this becomes a way to grow

Before any interviews: check what Materialspace's own data can already answer, such as which manufacturer most of your dealers buy from.

What could go wrong

  • Reading mistakes that look correct, and surface months later
  • Dealers seeing it as a way for manufacturers to go around them
  • Manufacturers not wanting to do setup work this early
  • 2020 adding automated quoting to the catalogs it already has
  • Manufacturers worried about relying on us: their data, their name on quotes, no paid ranking
